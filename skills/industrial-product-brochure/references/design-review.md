# Design Review and Revision Triage

Use this reference when a brochure draft looks acceptable at first glance but still feels weak, generic, or commercially unconvincing.

## Editorial Standard

A product brochure page must earn approval in four ways:

1. The product is truthful.
2. The buyer can recognize fit quickly.
3. The visual design has a deliberate catalog posture.
4. The page can survive A4 print and delivery.

Good taste is not enough. A nice page that hides the machine, muddies model differences, or reads like an instruction manual is still a failed product brochure page.

## Reader Behavior

Assume the reader flips fast.

- First glance: category, product shape, machine, or brand must register.
- Second glance: model difference, use case, or processing range must become clear.
- Third glance: specifications and contact details must be readable.

If the page requires explanatory copy to be understood, the layout is doing too little.

## Self-Rejection Checklist

Reject and revise before showing the user when any item is true:

- The largest visual element is not the commercial subject.
- The actual machine body is small because the white canvas was counted as image area.
- Samples look like diagrams when the page needs product photography.
- The page copies a web layout: oversized hero rhetoric, floating cards, CTA language, or marketing-site rhythm.
- The page copies a manual: long guidance text, procedural wording, or dense explanation that should have been visual.
- The design is symmetrical only because symmetry was easy, not because it clarifies the product.
- Bilingual copy is split into cluttered paragraphs instead of paired scan labels.
- The reference or design tool output is used as permission to ignore weak product communication.

## Revision Layer Model

When feedback arrives, identify the smallest layer that actually failed:

- **Truth:** wrong asset, model, logo, parameter, name, page order.
- **Scale:** machine, sample, or text too small for A4.
- **Material:** generated sample looks fake, schematic, or unrelated.
- **Communication:** page is pretty but does not explain fit, range, difference, or value.
- **Voice:** copy sounds like a guide, ad, tutorial, or internal note instead of a catalog.
- **Genre:** page grammar is wrong: cover, back cover, comparison, product hero, or delivery are confused.
- **Production:** export, page number, background patch, PDF, ZIP, bleed, or color proof issue.

Fix that layer first. Preserve accepted layers unless they depend on the failed layer.

## Rebuild Triggers

Do not polish a weak composition when:

- the page role is wrong;
- the machine cannot be made large enough without changing layout;
- the product/manual/web genre is confused;
- the user rejects the basic taste rather than a small defect;
- a previous local patch created visible artifacts.

In these cases, return to the manifest, rewrite the design brief, and rebuild the preview from the page role.

## Local Repair Rules

Small deterministic fixes can be excellent, but they must not look patched.

- Remove old marks with local background reconstruction, not a flat cover block.
- Match background tint, noise, gradient, and anti-aliasing.
- Check the repaired area at 100% and in a full-page contact sheet.
- Keep a backup before overwriting a progress or delivery file.
- Regenerate the PDF after any page image changes.

## Review Language

Use direct internal labels while working:

```text
Failure: scale layer
Evidence: primary machine prints around 35 mm wide
Action: enlarge machine, reduce sample strip, keep approved copy
```

For user-facing feedback, be shorter:

```text
I kept the approved layout logic, enlarged the machine, and reduced the supporting samples so the product can be inspected at A4 size.
```
